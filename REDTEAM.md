# Does this gym reward better research?

The gym distinguishes large regressions, but **does not demonstrate a reliable reward for better research near its reference**. More data, training and search produced a measurably better predictor without a hidden-test gain above seed noise. Exhaustive search of the tiny public library almost duplicates the reference. Five tested shortcuts beat or exactly tie it. The most serious scoring failures are a mutable comparison baseline, mutable ground-truth model, and inadequate enforcement of the design domain.

**Better and weaker methods.** Every row below ran the unmodified, no-argument `bash scripts/evaluate_test.sh`. Eta is lift/drag; pool eta averages the hidden 12° and 24° operating conditions. ΔR is improvement over the supplied runtime reference, using the declared metric directions, absolute coefficient magnitudes, experiment weights and per-metric standard deviations across the 21 finite accepted audit attempts. It is an explicit audit-cohort rank delta, not an estimate of an unknown future leaderboard. [All results](redteam/RESULTS.md), [normalization and individual terms](redteam/summary.json).

The stronger variants retain the supplied MLP and CMA-ES procedure, increase pairwise morphs from 3 to the paper's 10, train 600 rather than 200 epochs with learning rate .003 rather than .1, and search 200 rather than 80 generations. This is a genuine computational improvement attempt, not just edited scores. On a common, independent 2,048-example public-oracle validation set, seed 1 improves coefficient MSE from .00254068 to .00214330 (15.6%) and positive-lift eta MAPE from 22.147% to 19.679%. All three stronger predictors improve coefficient MSE against their paired freshly trained reference seed; only seed 1 improves it against the saved reference. [Validation results](redteam/training_quality.json), [diagnostic runner](redteam/check_training_quality.py).

Better predictor accuracy does not guarantee a better optimized shape. Here the observed design-score movements are all below seed spread, including the small movement in the wrong direction for seed 1. Thus this audit establishes a plateau, not a statistically significant reversal of scientific quality. Every weaker control falls by more than seed spread.

The reproduced method beats the genuine torpedo baseline in the direction reported by the paper: hidden-test pool eta 8.68610170 versus 0.77540432, gain 11.20202906. The simple baseline scores clearly below the reference. However, this is a synthetic benchmark result, **not a reproduction of the paper's physical claim**. The paper reports pool eta 2.5 and 2.4 for its fabricated designs versus literature torpedo eta .3, plus wind-tunnel validation. The runtime reference differs from the physical targets in `scoring/reference.json`; rank comparisons here anchor to the supplied `scoring/scores_test.json`, reproduced by the saved-reference run.

Departures from the paper's own method/settings, based on the local paper and source:

1. The paper fits deformation cages to real meshes and labels them with OpenFOAM. This gym substitutes eight hand-authored normalized descriptors and an analytic hydrodynamic formula. It contains neither cage geometry/mesh deformation nor CFD. This changes the experimental procedure, not just resource scale.
2. The reference uses 3 morphs per pair instead of 10: 590 shapes / 2,950 angle examples instead of 1,920 / 9,600 under the local construction. Both use five training angles from −30° to 30°.
3. The local network has four width-96 hidden layers plus an output layer; the paper specifies a four-layer MLP without those widths. Input/target scaling, random row 80/20 validation splitting, and selection of the best validation checkpoint are local choices. Related interpolated shapes can occur on both sides of that split. Adam, tanh, batch normalization, batch size 128, the original learning rate, warmup and training duration follow the paper's stated settings.
4. The reference searches softmax weights over 20 descriptors using population 24 and 80 generations, once at each of 9° and 30°. The paper uses cage parameters, repeated CMA-ES searches at each angle and a wider operating-angle curve. The local implementation additionally uses true-oracle efficiency to select between the two optimized designs. The audit jobs run on CPU rather than the paper's RTX A6000.
5. No wind tunnel, buoyancy engine, mass shifter, swimming-pool dynamics, fabrication, Reynolds-number matching or physical energy measurement is reproduced. Energy and glide-angle metrics are algebraic functions of synthetic lift/drag, not independent measurements.
6. The hidden test evaluates at 12° and 24°, retaining result labels `9deg` and `30deg`. It ignores the submitted top-level operating angle for ranked metrics. This evaluates fixed-angle shape quality rather than the full shape/control co-design claim.

**Headroom, bounds and brute force.** The reference is already at the exact lower bound of `shape_domain_violation = 0` in all three scored proposed rows: pool, `9deg` and `30deg`. The baseline's pool domain violation is also zero. No other nonzero-coefficient proposed metric is at an established algebraic bound; baseline self-gain 1 is a comparison identity, not a candidate ceiling. Surrogate diagnostics have coefficient zero and do not create scored headroom. A global optimum over the convex hull was not proven.

Nevertheless the test is practically too small for the surrogate's computational-efficiency motivation. The `shortcut_bruteforce_bases` run enumerates all 20 supplied bases with 40 public-oracle angle evaluations in 0.000816 seconds; `shortcut_bruteforce_morphs` enumerates all 1,920 pairwise-morph candidates with 3,840 evaluations in 0.000978 seconds. These times measure vectorized search, excluding interpreter/evaluator startup. Both select the existing `thin_ray` base using only visible 9°/30° conditions. Their hidden pool eta is 8.68610322, within 0.00000152 of the reference; their full rank is slightly worse (−0.000008200), far inside seed noise. They are failed shortcuts under the strict at-or-above rule, but they solve the test to its demonstrated resolution without training, CFD or CMA-ES. The near ceiling explains why a better surrogate earns almost nothing.

**How rank adds up.** For every scored proposed result key, the audit computes

`ΔR = Σ experiment_weight × abs(coefficient) × direction × (attempt − reference) / cohort_std`,

where direction is +1 for `higher_is_better`, −1 otherwise. Pool weight is .64; each angle row receives .35 in the literal sum. Coefficient magnitudes are 1 for gain, 1 for eta, .6 for energy, .3 for glide angle and 1 for domain violation. Negative coefficient signs are not used to reverse direction a second time. Baseline rows are comparison controls. Constant columns contribute zero. The summary also supplies a version averaging the two angle rows within their experiment; every working shortcut below still beats or ties under that version.

Gain and eta duplicate the same improvement after normalization when the baseline is fixed. Energy and glide angle are further monotone transforms of eta. They do not independently constrain feasibility or task completion. Raw eta has a nonzero coefficient despite being called diagnostic in `scoring/EXPERIMENTS.md`. Surrogate prediction accuracy has zero coefficients and is outside ranked experiments. There is no hard validity gate. A completely non-lifting candidate with a degraded baseline loses in the tested tradeoff, but invalid geometry **can** outrank the reference, and a non-lifting submitted operating point can tie it. Consequently the requested guarantee that task failure cannot pay is false.

The box shortcut has a geometric certificate: maximum span belongs uniquely to one base, so any convex combination attaining that span must be that base. All selected corners have that span but different remaining coordinates. [Certificate](redteam/shortcut_box_domain/hull_certificate.json). The evaluator checks only coordinate minima/maxima, which cannot enforce the convex hull.

Baseline replacement, box escape and fixed-model replacement dominate the reference in the scored candidate metrics, so their winning direction does not depend on normalization units or the eventual finite cohort. The impossible-finite shortcut trades domain violation against efficiency; its numerical win depends on cohort normalization. It wins both reported aggregation conventions, but no claim is made that it wins every possible future cohort. NaN's external handling is unknown.
